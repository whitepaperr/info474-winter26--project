Rising Tuition, Rising Debt
This is our INFO 474 final project: interactive article using P5.js.

College is often described as an investment in the future. But over the past two decades, the cost of that investment has grown dramatically — and the debt students carry has grown even faster.

In this interactive article, we use real national data from NCES and FRED to trace how tuition and student loan debt have changed since 2006, and what those numbers mean for students today.

Tuition over time
Public and private 4-year tuition both rise over time. We also plot the average to show the overall trend. Hover to see exact values by year.
Average tuition vs Total student loan debt
Here, we compare the average tuition trend with total U.S. student loan debt. Note the different units on the left vs right axis.
Year Comparison
Instead of reading every point on the time series, this view summarizes the change between two years. Select the start/end years. It shows the values for both years and the percent increase from start to end.
Which grew faster? (index gap)
This chart shows the difference between debt growth and average tuition growth each year (index-based). Values above zero mean debt grew faster than tuition.
YoY Change Heatmap
Darker cells mean larger year-over-year changes. This helps spot periods with especially large shifts. Hover a year to see the exact YoY values.
Your College Cohort
Pick the year you graduated (or plan to graduate). This chart highlights your four years on the tuition timeline and shows what you actually paid, what national student debt looked like when you walked across the stage, and how much it grew while you were in school.
